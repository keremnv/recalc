**Challenge to C and the strongest argument against changing the baseline.** C — CACHE EFFECT EXISTS BUT DOES NOT MATERIALLY ALTER BASELINE is acceptable as the preregistered decision category only if its immediate explanation says: “no proven P1 recoveries meet the preregistered material-baseline rule in retained archived ordinary submissions.” It is not an established absence-of-effect claim. The 41 unresolved P1 rows and reported 0–22.40% run/0–21.43% task bounds admit unresolved material effects. Retention is not random sampling, and those bounds do not cover unretained attempts. No confidence or universality claim follows from zero confirmed recoveries.

P1 also addresses a second refresh of already processed submissions. It cannot tell us what would happen to the different unrefreshed agent files. Replacing P1 with P2 after seeing their contrast would change the estimand; treating P1's lack of gains as a refutation of P2's raw-file sensitivity would make the opposite error. All retained P1 Template submissions resolve without material effect, which supports that specific scope, while raw research Template outputs can remain highly sensitive.

The strongest argument against a new historical ordinary baseline is that its scored boundary was already refreshed, all 183 returned historical tuples reproduce, no primary recovery is demonstrated, and another refresh causes one material harm. The positive corpus is selected, largely byte-unverified research output, with substantial unresolved or changed semantics and no demonstration of universal reference compatibility. Replacing the old numbers globally would conflate a newly chosen pipeline with correction of a historically wrong score. Preserve the archived baseline and identify any new research endpoint as a versioned system definition.

There is also a legitimate target-of-evaluation objection: if the task is to deliver an immediately usable saved workbook, valid cached outputs may be part of the artifact's quality. Evaluator-side assistance changes that target. If the target is formula/workbook logic under a declared calculation engine, leaving caches dependent on the writing library is an avoidable nuisance. The replay distinguishes these mechanisms but cannot choose the target on empirical gains alone. This is the strongest conceptual objection to calling one policy universally “fair.”

**Recalculation before scoring is warranted conditionally.** The positive reason is broader than recovering lost points. A uniform calculation stage prevents a writer's missing caches from hiding both correct formulas and formulas at expected-blank cells. Gate B's two concrete harm cells illustrate the latter: existing candidate formulas become visible and correctly lose blank agreement under the retained benchmark. Keeping gains and losses is therefore integral to the policy, not an exception to it. All arms must meet the same declared boundary, independent of score, task family or whether recalculation helps.

I support retaining the existing archived refresh stage and specifying the same stage for research endpoints intended to measure evaluated formula behavior, within a compatible declared engine domain. I challenge “YES — REQUIRED FOR REFERENCE-FAIR EVALUATION” if taken as a theorem that LibreOffice load/calculate/save is necessary or sufficient for every candidate. The experiment identifies effects of this pipeline, including serialization, formula rewrites and error-fallback behavior. It does not show that the references were generated under equivalent semantics, that a supported-feature detector proves compatibility, or that every operational RECALC_PASS preserves workbook meaning. The policy document itself correctly reserves compatibility for separate reference validation. Until that condition is established, the defensible claim is a declared normalized endpoint, not certified numerical fairness.

Freeze and version candidate processing, references and scorer together; retain the original artifact and reported technical/unsupported outcomes. References must not be opportunistically recalculated to remove an inconvenient harm, especially where golden cells are genuinely blank. Formula-error fallback remains an official-score convention, not proof that a numerical answer is correct. No silent fallback to raw scoring after refresh failure, no exclusion of regressing cases, and no engine promotion inferred from gain alone are justified. These are interpretation requirements for the proposed specification, not authorization for a new implementation or experiment in this phase.

**Recalculation before submission and the evaluation-only scaffold claim.** The evidence does not identify incremental score benefit from a second submission-side refresh once a controlled evaluation stage refreshes the same output. It supports one reliable calculation boundary; it did not randomize where that boundary sits or test an agent's use of readback before submitting. “ADD RECALC BEFORE EVALUATION ONLY” is consequently a reasonable minimum policy for the measured fixed-output evaluation problem, not an experimentally established optimal scaffold or a finding that submit/readback has no value.

For a usable user-facing workbook, submitting a calculated deliverable can have an independent rationale: downstream viewers may read caches, and pre-submission readback could reveal existing errors while the agent can still act. Those are plausible delivery/interaction benefits outside this replay's identified effect. Conversely, exposing recalculation to an agent would change the trajectory and possibly time/cost; no such improvement is established here. Do not infer either benefit or futility from this fixed-output experiment. Preserve an already functioning submission/archive refresh rather than adding duplicate exports; repeated recalculation is not established to be harmless or idempotent in score.

The accepted conclusion is therefore narrower than either universal rebaseline or blanket dismissal: retain the archived ordinary historical result; qualify the identified score-sensitive cases and incompatible endpoint claims; use one explicit supported calculation boundary for future comparisons whose target requires it; and keep submission/deliverable policy distinct from the demonstrated scoring effect. No old report or primary result needs erasure, no verifier is revived, and no further intervention is authorized by this review.
