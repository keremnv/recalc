# Formula-error feedback discovery: forensic diagnosis

## 1. Executive diagnosis

The registered verdict, `FORMULA_ERROR_FEEDBACK_INCONCLUSIVE`, is supported. Enriched treatment contact was 2/7 against the frozen ≥6/7 gate, and only 02_06 supplied a paired official outcome. Its modification delta was 0.0. Neither a benefit nor a capability failure is established.

The earliest dominant loss was **opportunity to deliver treatment**: a qualifying output save was absent in four uncensored enriched treatment trajectories, while the fifth uncontacted task was censored twice by the provider. Provider instability was serious (11/23 runs), but cannot alone explain the observed contact deficit. The two deliveries came at calls 38 and 40 of 50, after the agents' main workbook edits. This leaves little room for evidence gathering and correction.

A second, independent problem is what the report means. The detector compares input cells read as-is with a recalculated output; its 366/367 unchanged-formula error appearances show a *computational damage surface*, not a causal edit map. In 07_03, the shown circularity was already present in input formulas with cached numeric values. Feedback led to a targeted change in the circular chain and zero live errors, but official modification accuracy was ≈0.0029 and the run did not submit. In 02_06, the agent made no workbook edit after feedback and submitted all 30 reported errors.

The next action should be a small, newly preregistered **delivery and timing probe** on fresh discovery tasks. Preserve the report and detector, repair arm-neutral provider reliability, and make an early provisional save possible in both arms. This tests whether the same report can arrive with enough time to be used; it does not establish effectiveness under the original natural-save workflow.

## 4. Failure attribution

**H1, provider.** The 11 provider-censored runs include seven primaries and four replacements. Among the five enriched tasks without contact, only 04_09 has *both* treatment attempts provider-censored, so one missing task is directly attributable to provider failure. The censored 02_06 primary was replaced by a contacted treatment; its failure no longer explains a missing task. Under the observed behavior of all uncensored attempts, stabilizing 04_09 could raise contact at most from 2/7 to 3/7, short of 6/7. Other trajectories might change stochastically under different provider execution; their counterfactual contact is **UNKNOWN**, not creditable to provider repair. Provider instability also reduced valid control outcomes, notably for 07_03.

**H2, trigger opportunity.** Three uncensored enriched treatments (02_01 at the cost cap, 02_09 at the call cap, 10_05 with `NO_SUBMIT`) had no `output.xlsx` save. 10_10 saved at call 25, but the detector found zero new formula errors and no later save. These are four observed noncontacts not classified as provider censoring. A first *nonempty* save is a coherent natural product trigger only if ordinary agents both save and introduce detectable live errors in time. Here that surface was rare: six runs saved at all, and only two enriched treatment runs supplied a nonempty save. An arm-neutral instruction or checkpoint to save an interim workbook could raise opportunity while preserving the report's semantic contents and the **first nonempty save** rule; it would change the scaffold's natural behavior and therefore test assisted delivery, not the original natural-save surface. Delivering on a clean save, on submit, or without a save would change trigger semantics and create a new treatment identity.

**H3, timing.** Feedback reached 07_03 after call 40, leaving 10 calls, and 02_06 after call 38, leaving 12. 07_03 used all 10 remaining calls: four calculation/inspection calls, a workbook edit at 45, and five further checks; it never submitted. 02_06 used four bash checks and submitted at 43; it made no second workbook edit. Both had already written their main repair script before exposure. Earlier contact with the same report after a naturally earlier qualifying save would preserve the mechanism; forcing an earlier save changes the delivery regime. The frozen design is structurally susceptible to late contact because its first opportunity is a completed output write, after much inspection and editing.

**H4, representation/actionability.** Across saves, 366/367 finding appearances were at cells whose **formula text was unchanged**. The report lists error cells in sheet/address order, up to eight per sheet, without changed-cell flags, formulas, dependency paths, or causal priority. It can reveal a damaged region and sometimes a useful locus (`07_03!S52`), but does not itself identify the originating edit or distinguish a newly broken formula from a latent calculation problem exposed by save/recalculation. This is a representation limit, not evidence that the detector failed to observe live computational errors.

**H5, model response.** Both agents called bash immediately after delivery. Only 07_03 subsequently modified the workbook, and its six post-report formula edits target the circular chain. 02_06 examined reported-area values but did not modify them. There is no model-visible reasoning trace explaining either agent's interpretation of `Err:522`; “wrote immediately” is false for 02_06 and not, by itself, proof of useful contact for 07_03.

**H6, capability.** The single paired result is a modification tie, not a treatment benefit. The solo 07_03 score cannot be paired with a control and is low despite error disappearance. No observed regression guard breach establishes harm. Neither contacted anecdote warrants a mechanism-level capability verdict; lower token use or noncompletion cannot be counted as efficiency or benefit.

The findings divide into four causal buckets:

|---|---|---|
| **A. Pure experimental infrastructure** | Eleven request-deadline provider censors and missing paired workbooks. | Arm-neutral provider/worker reliability can be repaired under a new preregistration without changing the feedback mechanism. |
| **B. Treatment-delivery design** | Four usable enriched treatment attempts had no qualifying nonempty save; two contacts came after calls 38/40. | An arm-neutral early-save opportunity can deliver the same report sooner, but changes the agent's ordinary workflow and limits claims about natural product delivery. ## 5. 07_03 forensic reconstruction

At call 40 the agent's prewritten `/tmp/fix.py` loaded `input.xlsx`, made a broad set of workbook edits, and saved `output.xlsx` for the first time. Before feedback it had inspected workbook dumps and seen broken `#REF!` formulas, including LBO EBITDA row 55. The archived input had cached numeric values for LBO `S49`, `S50`, `S52`, and neighboring cells despite a formula cycle: `S52 = S53*AVERAGE(S48,S50)*7/12`, `S49 = S52-S51`, and `S50 = SUM(S48:S49)`. Thus the cycle was present in input formula text; the exact onset of the 36 live recalculation errors cannot be attributed to the agent's edits. The input was read as-is while the output was recalculated. At the triggering save the detector found 36 new `Err:522` cells on `LBO & Lenders`, with **0 changed formulas among the 36 findings**. The event recorded 41 input error cells and 44 current error cells; the 36 are a set difference, not the change in total errors.

The exact delivered text is preserved in [07_03 treatment_report.json](formula_error_feedback_discovery/runs/Debugging_07_03_TREATMENT/treatment_report.json): “A spreadsheet recalculation found newly introduced formula errors that were not present in the input workbook,” total 36 on `LBO & Lenders`, showing `S49`, `S50`, `S52`, `S56`, `S58`, `T48`, `T49`, `T50`, all `Err:522`, with a note that eight of 36 were shown. The wording says “newly introduced,” but the archived formula cycle predates the output save.

The first post-report action (call 41) recalculated/inspected the input via bash; calls 42–44 inspected output and input LBO values. The agent did not use `view_xlsx`, but did gather related evidence by another tool. At call 45 it changed **only six LBO interest formulas**, `S52:X52`, from interest on average opening/closing debt to interest on opening debt (`S53*S48*7/12`, then `T53*T48`, etc.). Of the eight shown cells, **S52 alone was edited**. `S49`, `S50`, `S56`, `S58`, `T48`, `T49`, and `T50` retained their formulas; their errors disappeared as downstream consequences. The explicit `S52→S49→S50→S52` dependency cycle is broken by the new `S52` formula. The second save at call 45 yielded zero live new formula errors; final recalculation recorded zero current errors, and all eight shown cells still had formulas. Calls 46–50 inspected values in LBO and Equity Returns, after which the run hit the call cap without `submit`.

The agent made 57 input-to-final cell-content edits overall, 51 in its pre-report save and six after feedback. The earlier edits included replacements for broken references and other finance formulas. The post-report row-52 edit plausibly caused the computed-error disappearance; it was not blank clearing. Whether interest on opening debt is the intended financial calculation, or whether other edits made the workbook semantically correct, is **UNSUPPORTED / UNKNOWN**. The only archived official aggregate is modification accuracy ≈0.0029, regression ≈0.9954, accuracy 0.0. It supplies no cell-level credit ledger explaining precisely which edits received credit. The control exhausted its cost budget without a scored workbook, so there is no paired effect. **Best supported classification: B, mechanical error suppression without *demonstrated* semantic repair**, with the qualification that “no semantic repair anywhere” cannot be proved from these artifacts. It is not A, and C remains an unverified possibility rather than an evidenced conclusion.

## 6. 02_06 forensic reconstruction

At call 38 the agent executed a prewritten script that saved `output.xlsx` once. Its five intentional edits repaired `Exhibit 9!N23:P23`, changed `Synergies!Q11`, `Merger Model!C15`, `DCF!G12:H12`, and a title typo. None were in the reported sensitivity grid. Re-saving also altered many cached sensitivity-table numbers by tiny numeric roundoff, but did not change those formulas. The detector reported 30 `Err:522` findings in `Berk-Hath Investment Package`, 0 with changed formulas. The exact [02_06 report](formula_error_feedback_discovery/runs/Debugging_02_06_TREATMENT_R1/treatment_report.json) showed `F10:F14` and `G10:G12` (eight of 30), with 12 calls remaining.

The first post-report action, call 39, recalculated a copy of the saved output and printed the reported-area sensitivity values. Calls 40–42 compared a recalculated input and output, enumerated cached errors through openpyxl, and spot-checked the preplanned edits. The agent called `submit` at 43. It neither used `view_xlsx` nor altered `output.xlsx` after the report. Its own openpyxl string scan printed “total 0,” but the frozen UNO diagnostic still found 30 live `Err:522` at final evaluation; the archived recalculated workbook has 30 `#VALUE!` cached cells in the same sheet. Those are different representations of a calculation problem, so the string scan is not a reliable negative check against the registered detector.

The shown `F10:F14` cells are unchanged rate-axis formulas; `G10` is an Excel data-table formula covering `G10:K14`, while `G11:G12` are table output cells. The input cached numeric values for them, whereas the recalculated output archived errors. The same 30-error pattern appears in the 02_06 control replacement and repeatedly in the 02_09 control. This supports a sensitivity-table/recalculation issue common to this workbook family; it does **not** establish the causal root or that the five treatment edits created those errors. Why the model accepted its own “total 0” check and submitted is **UNKNOWN**; the trace establishes that it inspected the area but did not resolve the registered errors. Official modification was 0.3188 in each arm (Δ0.0); regression was 0.9951 versus 0.9961 (Δ−0.001). The tie does not mean the agents made identical edits, and it provides no evidence that feedback improved capability.

## 7. Cascade / causal-localization analysis

The implementation in [frozen_runner.py](formula_error_feedback_discovery/frozen_runner.py) recalculates a copy of the current output, reads the input as-is, and passes the two cell maps to `new_formula_errors`. That check emits a finding where the output has a recognized formula error and the input cell does not have a recognized error. **“Changed”** then means the input and output *formula text* at the finding's address differ (`b != a` on the formula component); **“unchanged”** means they do not. It is not a count of all edited cells, of changed cached values, of independent causal events, or of unique error addresses. The 1 changed / 366 unchanged figure sums **367 finding appearances across save events**; repeated 02_09 control saves alone contribute repeated 30-error blocks. The sole changed appearance was the control's `#VALUE!` finding. Both delivered reports contained only unchanged-formula findings.

Recalculation can propagate a changed or newly exposed error into formulas that were never edited; such downstream symptoms are expected. Here, the input-as-is versus recalculated-output comparison also admits a second explanation: an existing dependency problem with a cached good input value can become visible on output recalculation. The input `07_03!S52` cycle and 02_06's data-table cells demonstrate why **99.7% should not be read as 99.7% harmless noise** or as proof that 99.7% are consequences of the agent's edits. The errors mark a damage surface and can focus attention on the affected sheet. They are poor standalone intervention targets: seven of eight shown 07_03 cells were not edited, and none of the eight 02_06 cells was edited. A model might benefit from seeing affected downstream cells, as `S52` was shown among them; the two contacts do not identify the average benefit. Filtering to directly changed formulas would have removed every delivered finding and destroyed the observed signal. Causal provenance or ranking root formulas would require additional dependency analysis and a materially new report/selection mechanism. The current detector exposes a **damage frontier**, not a validated causal frontier.

## 8. Earliest loss boundary

The first dominant broken edge is **usable trajectory → timely qualifying nonempty save → treatment contact**. It failed for four uncensored enriched treatments and was blocked by repeated provider censoring for a fifth. With contact at 2/7, the preregistered capability gate could not be evaluated. Provider execution is an earlier infrastructure boundary for 11 individual runs, but provider failure alone does not dominate the five-task contact deficit: repairing the one doubly censored enriched treatment would leave observed contact at no more than 3/7 if the other trajectories behaved as recorded.

Two later boundaries are independently evidenced. Both actual deliveries were late (calls 38/40). And the representation supplied error locations without causal attribution, with a potentially misleading “newly introduced” claim when recalculation exposed input-existing formula cycles or data-table behavior. These limits matter even if provider reliability is repaired; their capability cost remains unquantified because only two treatments contacted and only one official pair completed.

## 9. What the experiment actually taught us

**Positive evidence.** The frozen detector can produce a factual, reproducible live-error report after a save and append it without an extra model call. It detected visible computational failure in two treatment outputs; 07_03's report included a formula on a demonstrable circular path, and the agent subsequently broke that path. The 07_03 errors disappeared without clearing the shown cells. No observed pair breached the preregistered regression guard.

**Negative evidence.** Natural save opportunities were scarce, contact was late, and 02_06 did not make a post-feedback workbook correction. Error disappearance in 07_03 did not coincide with a strong official modification score or a submission. The current text does not explain dependency provenance, and its “newly introduced” phrasing is stronger than the as-is-input/recalculated-output comparison warrants.

**Unknowns.** The causal effect of feedback on official capability, harm in other tasks, the semantic correctness of 07_03's opening-balance interest change, whether an earlier report would alter behavior, and the exact causal origin of 02_06's table errors are **UNSUPPORTED / UNKNOWN**. Neither silence from the censored negative control nor the absence of a post-report `view_xlsx` call resolves these questions.

## 10. Mechanism status

`MECHANISM_REPRESENTATION_PROBLEM_INDICATED`

This is a mechanism diagnosis, not a change to the registered experiment verdict. A specific representation problem is evidenced: the displayed cells are mostly unchanged formulas, no causal provenance is shown, and “newly introduced” can describe a latent input formula cycle merely exposed by recalculation. The capability effect of that problem is untested. This status does not mean the detector is useless; it locates real computed damage and in 07_03 included a relevant cycle cell.

## 11. Smallest justified next experiment

**One experiment: a preregistered, paired delivery-and-timing probe on four fresh, zero-model-enriched discovery tasks, eight runs total.** Select the four by a fixed, gold-blind rule before any agent run; exclude these eight old tasks and the untouched confirmation reserve. Use the same model, tools, 50-call cap, instance cost cap, detector, exact report bytes, finding order/limits, one-episode rule, and first-*nonempty*-save trigger in treatment and invisible telemetry in control. Repair only arm-neutral provider execution (stable routing/deadline/retry handling, recorded before the first run). In **both** arms add one identical scaffold instruction: after the first substantive workbook edit, save a provisional `output.xlsx` by call 20 and continue auditing. A clean provisional save remains silent; the next nonempty save can still trigger. This is an assisted-delivery regime, explicitly not a natural-workflow replication. Freeze its prompt and all decision rules in a new preregistration.

1. **Scientific question:** Can the *same* report reliably contact the agent early enough for evidence gathering and a post-feedback workbook edit under an arm-neutral early-save opportunity, or do contact/actionability failures persist?
2. **Treatment held constant versus changed:** Report contents, detector, selection, wording, trigger semantics, and one episode remain constant. The common scaffold gains the provisional-save instruction; this changes exposure opportunity and baseline behavior in both arms.
3. **Infrastructure repair:** Provider routing/deadline/retry stabilization is logged and identical across arms; it creates no feedback content. No outcome-based replacement beyond one prespecified replacement for provider/runner/workbook-infrastructure censoring per arm/task.
4. **New mechanism identity:** Any change to contents, trigger semantics, error kinds, selection/order, or causal localization would require a separate variant and preregistration. The common save instruction defines a **new experimental delivery regime**, so its results cannot validate the original natural-save claim.
5. **Population:** Four previously unseen enriched tasks selected without agent-outcome information; paired control/treatment order randomized and frozen.
6. **Required contact:** At least **3/4** treatment tasks must receive the unchanged report by call 25 with at least 25 calls left. This is a feasibility criterion for this small probe, not a post hoc replacement for the old ≥6/7 gate.
7. **Primary endpoint:** Number of tasks meeting that timely-contact criterion, plus, conditional on contact, whether the agent inspects a reported cell or a traceable precedent/dependent and makes a relevant workbook edit before submit/cap. Count actual `output.xlsx` mutations, not bash calls.
8. **Capability/safety endpoint:** Exploratory paired official modification and regression deltas on valid submitted pairs; live-error change and shown-cell resolution as mechanism outcomes. Do not infer capability from unpaired scores or noncompletion.
9. **Censoring:** Preserve the frozen distinction: provider/runner/workbook infrastructure errors are censored; call/cost exhaustion, no save, clean save, and no submission are model/delivery outcomes. Report each denominator. Do not replace a run for an unfavorable score or missing natural save.
10. **Stopping:** Run exactly the four frozen pairs and allowed infrastructure replacements; stop without adaptation. Do not expand or tune from an interim contact count.
11. **Branch-closing result:** With ≥3 timely contacts, no traceable evidence use or relevant edits and no positive paired modification signal would justify closing this *unchanged report* branch as an actionable capability route in the assisted regime. If timely contact still fails, close the natural-save delivery claim pending a new treatment identity; do not pronounce the report ineffective conditional on contact.
12. **Result justifying later confirmation:** Timely contact on ≥3/4, traceable evidence use and semantic repair in multiple contacted cases, positive paired modification effects with no safety breach, followed by a separately preregistered discovery test of the intended delivery regime at adequate contact. This four-task probe alone cannot open the confirmation reserve.

## 12. What must not change

Freeze `new_formula_errors`, input/output recalculation protocol, `Err:522` and other recognized error kinds, exact report template, first nonempty-save condition, sheet/address ordering, eight-per-sheet and 80-total limits, one report per trajectory, report append position, model-visible tools, model, cap, scoring, safety thresholds, and old registered verdict. The fresh probe's arm-neutral provisional-save wording must be identical in control and treatment and frozen before inference. The old eight tasks remain diagnostic evidence only; the confirmation reserve remains untouched.

## 13. What would constitute a new mechanism identity

Changing report contents or model-visible wording; reporting directly changed cells preferentially; adding formula text, dependency ancestry, causal localization, severity filtering, new error kinds, priorities, or a different finding order; changing when a clean save, nonempty save, submit, or no-save state produces a report; or changing the number of report episodes creates a **new treatment identity**. Such a variant needs a new preregistration and fresh discovery population before confirmation. Arm-neutral provider/worker fixes do not by themselves change treatment identity. An arm-neutral provisional-save instruction changes the tested **delivery regime** and the natural behavioral surface even though the treatment rule and content stay frozen; its outcomes cannot be merged with the original eight-task estimate.

## 14. Decision table

| Plausible fresh-probe result | Scientific conclusion |
|---|---|

## 15. Final recommendation

`RUN_DELIVERY_TIMING_PROBE_BEFORE_RETRY`

The first experiment never achieved the contact needed for a capability test, and provider repair alone cannot explain four uncensored missing contacts. A four-task fresh probe with the original report and an arm-neutral early-save opportunity is the smallest way to learn whether the same feedback can arrive while the agent still has time to use it. Its common workflow change must be reported as assisted delivery. The original verdict remains `FORMULA_ERROR_FEEDBACK_INCONCLUSIVE`; the reserve and product claims remain untouched.
