# Second independent read-engine architecture/performance review

**Status:** architecture and evidence review only. No product, experimental implementation, frozen evidence, public claim, or presentation file was changed; no scored benchmark was rerun. Primary sources are the [Phase-1](READ_ENGINE_PHASE1_REPORT.md), [Phase-2](READ_ENGINE_PHASE2_REPORT.md), [Phase-3](READ_ENGINE_PHASE3_REPORT.md), [Phase-4 audit](PHASE4_OPTIMIZATION_DISCOVERY_AUDIT.md), and [Phase-4 result](READ_ENGINE_PHASE4_REPORT.md), together with their raw/profile/analysis ledgers and the [Phase-4 parent](../../read_engine_phase4/harness.py), [integrity gate](../../read_engine_phase4/parent_artifact.py), [Phase-3 child/proxy](../../read_engine_phase3/runtime.py), [safe artifact](../../read_engine_phase3/safe_artifact.py), [direct decoder](../../read_engine_phase1/prototype.py), and [unchanged admission](src/librecalc_agent/_frozen/eligibility.py) code. New diagnostic work was limited to arithmetic over existing Phase-4 rows, read-only inspection of the three merged-cell scripts/classifier, and 20 fresh-interpreter `import openpyxl`/bare-Python probes. The import probes were unscored, changed no evidence, and had uncontrolled OS cache.

**Evidence vocabulary:** **measured** means a Phase-4 paired full-command or instrumented component result; **code-demonstrated** means a concrete current call path; **inference** connects measured values without a direct stopwatch for that term; **hypothesis** requires a new causal test. “Faster” throughout means lower complete-command wall on the fixed 22 scripts unless another boundary is explicitly named. The 22 scripts represent 14 tasks, reuse identical workbook bytes across several rows, and were originally eligible/contact-selected; no result here establishes representative product prevalence or broad openpyxl equivalence.

## CURRENT SYSTEM STATE

The fixed primary population is exactly the old RC's 22 script/workbook identities. Old RC cold median was **2.017×** direct Python; Phase 3 cold **1.595×**, warm **1.230×**. Phase 4's same-run comparison established a real H1/H0 warm improvement (**0.704×**, 22/22 faster) by removing parent semantic artifact reconstruction and eager builder imports. H1/PY warm was **0.833×**, 13/22 faster and 9/22 slower, with exact outputs and child-confirmed reuse. The preregistered bootstrap median upper bound was **1.205**, so aggregate warm speed remained formally unsupported. H1/PY cold was **1.589×**, 22/22 slower. These are separate observed endpoints; the historical and new medians are not a same-run causal contrast.

At warm invocation the current shape is `external caller → fresh Python LibreCalc parent → fresh Python script child`. The parent imports config/classifier/capture, hashes the source, integrity-checks an artifact, snapshots workbooks, launches the child, snapshots/validates effects, and writes diagnostics. The child runs `sitecustomize`, imports `runtime → safe_artifact → prototype → openpyxl/lxml helpers`, imports real openpyxl to patch `load_workbook`, rehashes the source, fully decodes the artifact to a `MemoryBook`, serves supported proxy reads, and materializes normal openpyxl on escape. CONTROL is one fresh interpreter running the ordinary script. Successful direct construction/serving still never calls `openpyxl.load_workbook`; importing the openpyxl library remains part of the current child path.

The Phase-1/2 result constrains this review: direct OOXML matched all 51 archived exact traces and all checked cells on 38 reference-loadable books; the 39th remains reference-oracle-unavailable. Persistence and freshness worked across fresh processes. Phase 3 repaired the old four fixed semantic failures. These prove the narrow read mechanism, not full object/API exactness. Phase 4's new warm win does not make the cold build or all supported invocations economical.

## WHY THE 13 WINNERS WIN

All 13 H1/PY warm winners avoided reference fallback. Their median direct-Python control wall was **579 ms**, H1 wall **243 ms**, source workbook **576 KiB**, artifact **154 KiB**, child artifact load **86 ms**, and logged direct-read count **715** (per-workload medians; ranges are broad). The nine losers' corresponding descriptive medians were control **151 ms**, H1 **178 ms**, source **56 KiB**, artifact **30 KiB**, child load **16 ms**, direct reads **609**; four had fallback. Bigger artifacts cost more to load but often replace much more expensive normal workbook execution. Read count alone is not the explanation: Financial_Model_11_02 wins on 111 or 272 logged direct reads, whereas a short Template with 1,057 reads still loses.

The most informative matched-workbook contrasts are **inferences**, not direct control parse timers. On the same 08_03 workbook, a fallback sibling recorded a normal reference parse of ~**2,162 ms** while a no-fallback sibling's control wall was ~2,749 ms and H1 artifact load ~711 ms; avoiding normal parse has room to pay for the artifact. On 15_03, a fallback sibling's ~**203 ms** normal parse compares with ~64 ms direct artifact load and a no-fallback sibling winning 227 versus 342 ms. On Debugging_05_02, a fallback sibling's ~**502 ms** parse helps explain why a no-fallback sibling wins 209 versus 905 ms. The scripts differ, so these logged parse times cannot be inserted as exact components of the siblings' control runs. Other large winners (08_01 and 07_01) have controls ~1–2.7 seconds and H1 warm walls ~0.27–0.89 seconds. The Phase-1 index-ready trace result already showed that much of the gain comes from avoiding **normal workbook acquisition/parsing**, not necessarily faster individual cell property dispatch.

## MERGED-CELL FALLBACK REASSESSMENT

The three merged cases are concrete. `Financial_Model_08_03__59b98508fd79.py` evaluates `ws.cell(row=r, column=c).value`; its non-`None` branch then requests `.coordinate`, but covered merged cells produce `None`, so that branch does not observe their coordinate. Its frozen sheet has **38** covered-merge coordinate contacts in the loop. `Debugging_01_06__7b42a0f86b41.py` and `Debugging_05_02__9e6b464d2158.py` put `ws.cell(...).value` in printed list comprehensions; they touch **6** and **8** covered-merge coordinates respectively. The exact proxy route is `ProxyWorksheet.cell` → test `SheetInfo.merged` → `_real_sheet()` → cached `ProxyWorkbook._real_workbook()` → normal `openpyxl.load_workbook` once. Phase-1 `MemoryBook.cell` already knows the narrow covered-merge `value=None`, `data_type='n'` result. The Phase-4 same-run normal parse medians are **53, 502 and 2,162 ms**, and each affected H1 script still loses.

This is a high-leverage **fixed-population** candidate, but the current classifier is not the required semantic proof. Read-only diagnostic classification of ordinary snippets showed `A1_ADMIT` for `print(type(ws.cell(...)))`, `print(repr(ws.cell(...)))`, and `for ws in wb`. The last case already falls back; the first two demonstrate that admission does **not** certify only `.value`/`.data_type` observations of the returned object. Even on a non-merged cell, `type(proxy_cell)` differs from real openpyxl `Cell`. Merely changing covered cells to `ProxyCell` would silently widen that risk. The current `MergedCell` class has fixed `value=None`, `data_type='n'`, row/column slots, and a representation derived from sheet title/coordinate. Constructing a genuine `MergedCell` against the proxy worksheet may preserve type/value/coordinate/repr cheaply on the observed scripts, but style and other inherited behavior can still depend on the real worksheet. We have **not** proved a general exact drop-in object. A safe experiment needs explicit observation certification or a verified compatible object with fail-closed richer access; simply suppressing fallback is not justified.

An impossible zero-cost subtraction of those three logged parses from H1 warm medians yields median ratio **0.634** to direct Python and **15/22** faster. This is a diagnostic ceiling, not a prediction: construction of a compatible object and any remaining rich access have costs and semantic obligations. The fourth fallback is different: `for ws in wb` needs workbook/worksheet iteration outside the narrow contract and logged ~203 ms reference parse. A merged-cell change does not address it. The prior recommendation therefore targets a real pathology, but it does not answer whether the runtime shape is efficient on short scripts.

## SHORT-SCRIPT OVERHEAD REASSESSMENT

The three Template controls are **93–101 ms**; H1 full commands **154–158 ms**. Per-script H1 child wall is **~95–97 ms**, essentially the control wall. The remaining **~58–59 ms** is visibly outside that child: `external wall − parent_total` **~52 ms**, plus `parent_total − child_wall` **~6 ms**. Artifact load is only **0.5–0.8 ms**, parent integrity validation ~0.1 ms, no fallback, and after-run capture on these unchanged files is small. Faster JSON or direct-cell dispatch cannot plausibly remove 57–62 ms here.

The ~52 ms outside `parent_total` is more localized than “process startup.” H1's own timers locate **~16.6–16.8 ms** from module entry to `run()` (top-level imports/CLI), then **~15.5–15.6 ms** for RC config/classifier/capture imports before `parent_total` begins. The remaining **~19–20 ms** includes interpreter entry before the module timer, argument/launch details not separately clocked, final profile write, shutdown and external reaping; **its split is UNMEASURED**. Parent work *inside* `run()` but outside the child is ~6 ms, containing preflight, admission, source hash, artifact integrity, capture and diagnostics. A 20-observation diagnostic fresh interpreter probe in the frozen RC venv measured bare Python **10.4 ms** and `import openpyxl` **89.0 ms** median; uncontrolled cache and no workload script. CONTROL's ~93–101 ms on tiny Templates is consistent with import-dominated execution, while treatment's child bootstrap ~66–68 ms plus child startup residual ~27 ms makes its child wall similar. Openpyxl import is largely **common work**, not a free 66–89 ms treatment-only saving.

This decomposition has an architectural sting: deleting the extra *interpreter* alone cannot be credited with all ~58 ms. A one-process Python launcher must still import configuration/capture/admission, check freshness, install interposition, and perform post-run observation. Roughly 32 ms of pre-`run` imports are currently in the wrapper and could remain unless the command is made genuinely thinner. Conversely, a normal child already costs about what CONTROL costs, so the extra LibreCalc parent is the direct cause of the short-script loss in the **current** implementation. Which part of its cost a different architecture can actually avoid is the key unresolved causal question.

The process question is now legitimate. Phase 4 removed the obvious parent duplicate work yet five no-fallback scripts still lose solely or nearly solely at the wrapper boundary. A process-model experiment would resolve a broader architectural uncertainty than the merged-cell repair. But the current data **do not** prove that one-process execution will preserve effect assurance or save the whole wrapper cost.

## OPENPYXL IMPORT REASSESSMENT

Current CONTROL and H1 child scripts import real `openpyxl`; H1 `Runtime.install` imports it before the script, and `safe_artifact → prototype` also imports openpyxl formula/date/number-format utilities. Fresh diagnostic `import openpyxl` cost ~89 ms including interpreter wall; bare Python ~10 ms, so package initialization is substantial. Much of H1 child bootstrap overlaps exactly that common CONTROL work. Merely delaying `Runtime.install`'s import until the script executes moves a stopwatch boundary, not necessarily command wall.

Could treatment avoid real openpyxl on a direct-only path? **In principle yes, but only with a much larger compatibility surface.** A shadow module/import hook could expose `load_workbook` backed by direct state and import the real package on unsupported access. To make it actually save ~60–80 ms it must also prevent the serving decoder/artifact loader from importing real openpyxl; current formula observable classes, date helpers and formula translation violate that requirement. It must handle `from openpyxl import Workbook`, submodule imports, `isinstance`/`type`, module attributes, import order, already-imported modules, and fail-open delegation without changing names/classes or double-executing code. The current classifier does not certify import forms or all object observations. A shim could be powerful on the tiny Templates, but may cost more semantic scope than the narrow read engine originally earned. It is **hypothesis/high risk**, not a free startup optimization.

## PERSISTENT ARTIFACT REASSESSMENT

H1 parent validation is almost free; the child still reads, decompresses, parses, validates and constructs the full `MemoryBook`. Child artifact load is **0.5–0.8 ms** on the three Templates, **~16–18 ms** on the two other no-fallback losers, **~12–18 ms** on two small merged losers, **~61 ms** on iteration fallback, and **~672 ms** on the large merged fallback. It also reaches ~677–711 ms on several **already decisively winning** large model scripts. Therefore a flat/mmap/per-sheet/SQLite file-backed reader would primarily make big winners faster; it could flip the near-break-even `Financial_Model_18_05` if enough of its 16 ms disappears, but cannot by itself fix the ~57–62 ms Templates or the 53–2,162 ms reference parses. For the large merged loser, zero-cost removal of ~672 ms load still leaves roughly +70 ms excess and the normal parse. Phase 2's simple SQLite was sometimes faster on large snapshots because it avoided full object reload, but that is not a measured Phase-4 full-command alternative and does not authorize a new format choice by headline.

Cold remains negative (**1.589×**, 22/22 slower) because direct OOXML full-workbook build, safe serialization/publication, wrapper and child run are all charged. Demand-scoped cells or per-sheet artifacts could reduce work on a first invocation, especially when scripts read few cells. A safe design would derive metadata and then build on *actual* accessed sheets/cells, with dynamic access and fallback intact. Static literal-cell lists from source are insufficient: loops, computed coordinates and conditional reads are part of ordinary Python, and the harness must not predict agent intent. Direct source XML/package offset caching, lazy sheet decode and reference-first adaptive materialization are valid future hypotheses, but broaden storage/semantic engineering. Replacing `JSONZ_MEMORY_V1` with mmap, flat typed records or per-sheet files may be worthwhile **after** the process/fallback bottleneck is understood, not as the next experiment for the nine current losers.

## MINIMUM VIABLE RUNTIME

The minimum honest capability set is: accept the unchanged Python/openpyxl script; choose direct serving only for supported calls; keep exact reference fallback; bind a persistent artifact to whole-file source identity and code/contract versions; fail closed on corruption/staleness; and observe pre/post workbook effects. For a normal-exit, single-invocation conceptual minimum: one fresh Python interpreter can perform config/admission/freshness, pre-snapshot, install the interposer, execute the script, run the child-equivalent semantic gate on first load, then post-snapshot/validate and emit a compact receipt. One semantic artifact decode is enough. It may still need a **separate supervisor** to guarantee post-run observation after abnormal termination or to preserve exact direct-script process semantics. That distinction is the architectural fork.

Current H1 has a fresh Python parent, fresh Python child, environment JSON context, sitecustomize import, two source hashes, pre/post package snapshots, UUID run directory, detailed events and four per-run JSON writes. Some of these exist for safety: source SHA, child recheck across the parent→child race, child artifact decode and reference fallback. Some are desired product observability: capture/delta and a durable diagnostic summary. Others are prototype choices: detailed per-phase timers, event volume, `last_run` and duplicate profile writes, captured child stdout/stderr re-forwarding (the public RC runner normally inherits streams), and experimental context names. The Phase-4 parent/child **division** is not intrinsic to narrow read substitution; it is inherited from the current product-shaped harness and its assurance strategy. LibreOffice/UNO is absent from the direct read path; old `candidate_a`/`substrate` naming and broad historical index assumptions are lineage, not current read-engine requirements. No rename is proposed.

An intentionally asymmetric product could have a certified direct-only path, a hybrid direct/reference path, and a reference-only path. That is credible only if the certificate specifies observable operations and the hybrid path preserves full fallback. The unchanged whole-script classifier is too weak to be a blanket rich-object certificate; per-load and per-operation checks are already doing real semantic work. Reference-only scripts (22/30 Phase-3 secondary) currently pay prototype bootstrap/telemetry, making a near-direct path valuable for representative economics, though it has **zero leverage on the fixed 22 primary scripts**. The classifier should not be treated as a workload-ranking oracle.

**Dominance/selection filter.** Strict Pareto domination is uncommon because semantic risk and engineering cost trade against reach. The **full native OOXML decoder rewrite** (#13, full-decoder variant) is dominated *for the current warm-nine question* by its own smaller native-reader variant: the decoder is not called on valid warm reuse, so a full rewrite removes no additional warm work, has larger risk/complexity and is less isolatable. The simple `execve` variant (#16) fails the required post-capture property; adding an observer makes the explicit native-supervisor idea (#6) a clearer, at-least-as-honest form of the same design. Micro-trimming (#15), proxy dispatch (#14), and cold demand-scoping (#10) are **not formally Pareto-dominated**—they target distinct costs or have lower risk—but are filtered from the immediate warm-nine decision for small measured ceilings or a different endpoint. Reference-only routing (#11) is a strong secondary-product experiment, but primary 22 contact by construction. No candidate is declared dominated merely because it is harder or less fashionable.

## CANDIDATE PARETO FRONTIER

The immediate non-dominated choices are: **merged-cell compatible serving** (#1: large localized savings, lowest process disruption, real semantic proof gap); **one-interpreter architecture probe** (#3: broad fixed-cost question, difficult process/assurance semantics); **persistent parent/fresh child** (#4: safer script isolation, service/IPC economics and deployment burden); and **deferred real-openpyxl import** (#7: potentially broad import saving, largest API-compatibility surface). **File-backed artifact reads** (#9) remain on a *large-workbook* frontier but not the residual-nine center: one large fallback loser might improve, while most current losers load in <18 ms. **Demand-scoped cold construction** (#10) remains on the *first-use* frontier because 22/22 cold scripts still lose. There is no honest single numerical rank across those different questions.

The frontier exposes a real tradeoff. A merged-cell repair may improve the fixed population's median and majority quickly yet leave an extra-process tax on small ordinary scripts. A one-process result could show that the current parent is unnecessary, but may fail the abnormal-exit assurance contract. A daemon retains isolation but introduces a service and a new session boundary. The shim might save an entire library import but enlarges the semantic replacement well beyond the proven read contract. Experiment choice should maximize **decision value**, not just expected benchmark gain.

## BEST-CASE CURRENT ARCHITECTURE

Diagnostic counterfactuals below subtract measured **per-workload warm component medians** from per-workload H1 command medians, hold all other work constant, and recompute paired ratios. They are deliberately impossible zero-cost bounds, **not predictions or new benchmarks**. `A` removes only the three merged-cell reference parse timers (53/502/2,162 ms); workbook iteration remains. `B` removes *all* measured parent in-`run()` non-child work (~6–13 ms), even though some is required for admission, freshness, capture and diagnostics. `C` removes *all* external-minus-parent residual (~48–63 ms), even though some startup/import work would relocate in any real design. A and B leave the two-process shape; C crosses a process/lifecycle boundary. Component medians are nested and non-additive, so combined rows are rough optimistic accounting, not physically exact stopwatch subtraction.

## BEST-CASE ARCHITECTURAL REDESIGN

If a one-interpreter design could keep the **observed H1 child wall** and eliminate every separately measured parent/wrapper millisecond without relocating anything, the diagnostic child-wall/CONTROL median would be **0.545**, with **17/22** faster. On the three Templates the child/control differences are approximately **+3.5, −5.6 and −1.2 ms**: two are already faster *inside* the child and one nearly tied. This is a generous lower bound, not an expected single-process result. Source freshness, admission, pre/post capture, semantic artifact validation and diagnostics still need to execute somewhere. With the current Python launcher, ~16–17 ms top-level/CLI imports and ~15–16 ms RC imports are not automatically removed by `runpy`. The parent has ~19–20 ms of unexplained external residual; the child has ~27 ms of startup residual. A one-interpreter design could eliminate **one** interpreter startup path and the handoff, but it cannot claim the sum of both residuals as savings, and neither residual cleanly isolates startup from other work. Their realizable saving is **UNMEASURED**. A persistent parent similarly pays client/IPC and must charge service startup over an explicit session horizon.

The larger redesign question is therefore **not** “can we subtract 58 ms from every row?” It is whether a thinner one-interpreter command can preserve all six minimum-runtime properties while keeping its total additional work below the small control parse/read savings on short scripts. The upper bound shows potential; the measured import obligations make success uncertain. A direct full-command A/B plus abnormal-exit semantic gate is the only responsible way to answer it.

## HAS THE CURRENT ARCHITECTURE HIT A LOCAL OPTIMUM?

**Not a performance optimum**, because three merged-cell fallbacks are known full reference parses and some parent bookkeeping is still nonessential. On the fixed population, an exact merged-cell route could materially improve the central result. **Possibly a shape limit for short scripts**, because their child already matches direct Python while the extra parent costs ~58 ms. Artifact format, cell dispatch and native decoding cannot remove that fixed wrapper. Phase 4 made this separation visible: the remaining losses are pathology-specific reference work **and** broad fixed runtime work. Polishing the former may make the chosen 22 look faster without showing whether ordinary tiny workbook scripts can pay for the product boundary. Replacing the latter may sacrifice the very isolation and post-run assurance the boundary provides. Both hypotheses deserve one discriminating test, kept separate.

## NEXT LOW-SURFACE EXPERIMENT

**Hypothesis:** the three covered-merge reads can use an object that is exact for the admitted observations, avoiding their normal reference parse while preserving fallback for richer behavior. **Change:** in an offline H1-derived arm only, construct a genuine or observationally compatible merged-cell result from existing merge metadata; add a *pre-timing* admitted-language/object-observation gate proving the returned object cannot silently escape the frozen contract. If that cannot be proved with the current classifier, conservatively tighten admission/certification and record every changed admission/contact status; do not quietly force the same 22 to contact. Keep workbook iteration and all other fallback unchanged. This is one merged-cell semantic hypothesis, not a format/process optimization.

**Invariant comparison:** exact same 22 script/workbook identities, direct-Python control and full-command cold/warm/N=1,2,3,5 session clocks; H1 remains the same-run optimization comparator. **Semantic gate:** all 22 outputs/package state exact; type, repr, coordinate, value, data_type, style/rich access, aliases and object escape fixtures for merged and ordinary cells; unchanged non-merged fallback; fail-closed reference on uncertain observation. Preserve child-confirmed reuse. **Falsification:** no safe general rule under admitted scripts, an admission/contact change that prevents the intended causal comparison, any new semantic mismatch, or little/no full-command improvement after reference parses actually disappear. **Decision enabled:** whether a localized proxy escape is worth integrating into a narrow contract, or whether reference materialization must remain the honest price for merged-cell identity. The expected ceiling is from logged parse times; it is not a claim that three workloads will cross break-even.

## NEXT HIGH-LEVERAGE EXPERIMENT

**Hypothesis:** a one-interpreter, full-command runtime can provide ordinary script interposition, freshness, fallback and effect observation with materially less fixed overhead than H1's fresh Python parent plus fresh Python child, without altering the narrow decoder/artifact. **Change:** build a separate offline one-interpreter arm that performs H1's config/admission/hash/artifact check and pre-snapshot, installs the same proxy/reference route, executes the unchanged script with `runpy.run_path(..., run_name='__main__')` or a demonstrably equivalent mechanism, then performs the same post-snapshot/validation/receipt. No shim, new format, merged-cell change, or population change in this test. Keep each invocation a fresh Python process and charge all setup/exit work. Treat exact direct-`python` process semantics as a gate, not an assumption.

**Invariant comparison:** same 22 identities, same H1 artifact format/source SHA/fallback/capture, same direct-Python and H1 controls, external command launch→exit for cold, warm and observed N=1,2,3,5. Add explicit process-semantics fixtures for `sys.argv`, `sys.path[0]`, `__name__`/`__main__`, cwd, environment, stdout/stderr, `SystemExit`, exceptions, atexit workbook writes, signals, child subprocesses and abrupt termination; show what post-run capture can and cannot guarantee. No favorable full-command result is valid if an admitted script silently changes semantics or loses required observation. Instrument retained parent-like imports, artifact load, script wall, capture and shutdown so a gain is attributable to process shape rather than omitted work. **Falsification:** semantic/process-assurance gate fails, required product work relocates and eliminates the expected wall saving, or the fixed short scripts remain materially slower. **Decision enabled:** whether the extra Python parent is a removable implementation accident or the honest cost of the current assurance contract. If abnormal-exit assurance cannot coexist with one process, the result directs the next design toward a thin external supervisor/daemon instead of continued `runpy` polishing.

This architectural experiment has **higher decision value** than another representation shootout: it directly challenges the fixed overhead that no artifact reader can fix on the tiny losers. It should be a prototype/falsification test, not a premature product integration.

## INDEPENDENT RECOMMENDATION

If responsible for making this system fast, I would investigate **process ownership first**, with the one-interpreter experiment above, while keeping the merged-cell study as the next contained proxy experiment. Phase 4 already made H1 warm faster than H0 on all 22 and produced an observed H1/PY median below one; the remaining lack of formal aggregate support is a distribution and uncertainty problem, not proof that another cell-index format is needed. Three losers are expensive merged-cell fallbacks and deserve a precise semantic repair. But five others expose a repeatable ~58 ms wrapper tax even when the child itself matches or beats CONTROL. Learning whether that tax is intrinsic to effect assurance is the architectural decision with the broadest product consequences. I would not commit to merging a local fallback optimization—or to a daemon, shim, native rewrite or new artifact format—until that process-boundary question has a falsifiable answer on the same scripts and full command timer.
