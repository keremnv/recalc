## PHASE 10 TAX REPLICATION

**`PHASE10_TAX_NOT_REPLICATED`.** In the CPU-pinned primary ladder, unchanged P4−P0 was **+6.39 ms** median per-workload paired second invocation (95% workload bootstrap interval **[+5.63, +9.71] ms**), ratio **1.037**, 1 faster/21 slower. This does not identify the exact cause of Phase 10's +18.85 ms result. ## CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION VERDICT

**`TAX DID NOT REPLICATE`.** The current, unchanged product met the frozen engineering budget in two pinned same-run comparisons. The observed fixed overhead is mostly the negative bootstrap/config/routing composite, with a small observer envelope; neither explains an unavoidable +18.85 ms tax in this controlled condition. The unpinned Phase-10B runs remain visible and show why host timing identity matters. This result supports a controlled-environment validation, not a universal millisecond guarantee on every host.

## AGENT INDEPENDENT MAINTAINER OPINION

I would stop chasing these reference-only milliseconds for now. The retained product pays roughly +6 ms on this controlled host while preserving an observer that survives script death; that contract remains worth the measured cost. P5 demonstrates a small possible improvement, but duplicating classifier rules across modules is a maintenance hazard for no required budget gain. The next valuable engineering work is release-readiness and cross-host validation of the retained architecture, with a separate policy for how practical budgets are checked on heterogeneous CPUs. I would not propose a higher budget from these data. Simplifying the experimental timing and receipt scaffolding before shipping would improve maintainability more than another local speed tweak.
